RL regression: isolate the update

This is an off-policy fixed-trajectory development ablation. All treatments use identical sampled training-game positions and outcomes. It is not a fresh on-policy study or independent final test. Released models remain unchanged.

Mechanism and mathematics

The outcome-only objective fits realized returns from shallow self-play. These returns describe the generating players, not optimal chess. A useful Stockfish-trained estimate may disagree with those outcomes. The anchored objective is mean((v−z)²) + λ mean((v−v₀)²), with a detached frozen initializer v₀ and λ=1. Its unconstrained per-position optimum is (z+λv₀)/(1+λ), explicitly trading outcome fitting against preservation of initial estimates.

Baseline teacher-validation MSE 0.241584; prediction standard deviation 0.6827. A lower teacher error here measures preserved agreement, not proven playing strength.

Why return targets can pull values toward zero

Among sampled positions where the initializer has |value| ≥ 0.75, about 22–24% lead to draws and 6% lead to an opposite-sign result in shallow self-play. These are valid outcomes, not demonstrated label errors. They illustrate that learning these players’ outcomes is a different objective from preserving strong-engine estimates.

Intervals resample shared opening pairs jointly across the three fixed seeds, conditional on these trained models.

Interpretation and limitations

The reference fits outcomes while losing teacher agreement and compressing its prediction range. Smaller steps and anchoring reduce that drift. This supports update-induced drift as a controllable mechanism, but does not prove it is the sole cause of chess regression. Correlated states, shallow behavior-policy errors, a changing game distribution and repeated development-set use remain limitations. No new labels, architecture search, final games or deployed weights were used.
