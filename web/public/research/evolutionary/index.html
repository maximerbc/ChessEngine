What learns

A 63-input neural evaluator has 324 learned parameters: four ReLU units, a learned linear connection and a bounded scalar output. Counts, pawn structure, king safety and attack coverage are human-designed inputs. All evaluation weights start randomly; no fixed material score, Stockfish labels, pretrained weights or gradient updates enter training. Our alpha-beta search chooses moves.

How it learns

CMA-ES evaluates populations against Classical, the released Supervised evaluator and archived evolutionary opponents. Every candidate receives the same color-swapped positions and playing budgets within a generation. Historical opponents and changing curriculum make tournament fitness a development measure, not a stable strength rating.

Evidence and limits

0 recorded matches; paid compute C$0. No Elo or calibrated win-probability claim. Capped games retain unfinished rule results and are reported separately. Opening-pair uncertainty and variation between training seeds are different quantities.
